In three dimensions, each linear equation represents a plane.

The blue plane is x plus y plus z equals 6.

The green plane is 2x plus 3y plus z equals 11.

And the orange plane is x plus 2y plus 3z equals 14.

Two non-parallel planes intersect along a line. When the third plane crosses that line, all three meet at a single point: (1, 2, 3). This is our unique solution.

Why do row operations preserve this solution?

Adding a multiple of one row to another replaces a plane with a linear combination. The plane tilts, but it still passes through the common intersection line.

Row operations simplify our view without altering where the planes intersect.
